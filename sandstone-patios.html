Questions

Things people ask us

Does a sandstone patio need sealing?
We would recommend it. A good sealer keeps stains out and makes it far easier to clean. We will tell you which one and when.
Will a sandstone patio go green?
Anything in shade will pick up algae eventually. A wash down once a year keeps it right.
Should I choose a sandstone or porcelain patio?
Sandstone for character and warmth, porcelain for low maintenance and consistency. We will bring both and let you decide in your own garden.
How do you remove black spots from a sandstone patio?
Black spots on sandstone are usually lichen, and they need a biocide or specialist black spot treatment rather than scrubbing or pressure washing. The treatment kills the lichen and the marks fade over several weeks as the weather breaks them down. Blasting them off at high pressure damages the stone's face and they come back. Sealing the stone after treatment makes it harder for lichen to take hold again.
Is riven or sawn sandstone better for a patio?
Neither is better; they suit different gardens. Riven sandstone has a split, textured face and uneven thickness, giving a traditional look with good grip. Sawn sandstone is cut flat with crisp edges for a cleaner, more contemporary line, and it needs a textured or sandblasted finish to stay grippy when wet. Sawn stone costs a little more and shows algae sooner in shade.
Is Indian sandstone ethically sourced?
Not always. Much of the Indian sandstone sold in the UK comes from Rajasthan, where quarry labour standards vary and child labour has been reported. Ask the stone supplier for evidence of an audited, traceable supply chain, such as membership of the Ethical Trading Initiative or independent quarry audits, rather than just a label. We are happy to talk through where your chosen stone comes from.
Does sandstone change colour over time?
Yes. Sandstone weathers, so most colours mellow and soften over the first year or two, and some shades darken or pick up warmer tones, which is a large part of its appeal. Iron in some stones can show as small rust-coloured spots. A sealer slows the change and helps keep stains out, and a colour-enhancing sealer deepens the tone if you prefer the wet look.
Is a sandstone patio slippery?
Riven sandstone has a naturally textured face and is usually grippy, even when wet. Smooth sawn sandstone can be more slippery unless it has a textured finish. The real risk on any stone is algae: in shade, under trees or on a north-facing patio a green film builds up and makes the surface slippery, so a regular wash and occasional algae treatment keep it safe.
Why is my sandstone patio flaking?
Flaking, called delamination or spalling, happens when water gets into the natural layers of the stone and freezes, splitting off thin sheets. It is most common in softer or poorer quality stone, slabs laid with the bedding plane the wrong way up, and patios that hold water because they were spot-bedded or laid without enough fall. Badly flaking slabs cannot be repaired and need replacing.
How do you clean a sandstone patio?
Brush it with water and a stone-safe cleaner, and treat algae and lichen with a product made for natural stone. A pressure washer on a low setting, held well back, is fine for a light clean, but high pressure erodes the face of sandstone and strips out the joints. Avoid strong acid cleaners and bleach, which can mark the stone. Clean, dry stone can then be resealed.
How much does a sandstone patio cost in Milton Keynes?
A sandstone patio typically costs £130–£180 per m² laid on a full mortar bed, so a 25–30m² patio is roughly £3,250–£5,500. Riven or sawn finish, slab size, the amount of cutting, steps and walls, and any old patio to take up move the figure. Your fixed price comes in writing after the free design visit.
Is a sandstone patio cheaper than porcelain?
Usually a little, at £130–£180 per m² laid against £150–£200 for porcelain. The slabs often cost about the same; the difference is mostly in laying, because porcelain needs a bonding primer on every slab and diamond-blade cutting. Over time, sandstone costs a little more to look after, since it needs sealing and occasional cleaning to keep it looking its best.
