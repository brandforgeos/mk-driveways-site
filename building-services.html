What we take on

Small structural work, properly run

We are not a general builder and we do not want to be. What we do take on is the smaller structural work that sits naturally alongside groundworks: garage conversions, single storey extensions, porches and garden rooms.

The common thread is foundations, drainage and building regulations — which is exactly the territory we came from. We produce our own drawings, submit the building notice or full plans, meet the inspector on site and handle the sign-off.

If your project needs a structural engineer, an architect or a party wall surveyor, we will tell you at the visit rather than halfway through.

How we run it

Building control, done in order

  1. Survey and measureA full measured survey with a written record sheet, photographs and levels. This is where most problems get found.
  2. DrawingsWe produce the plans, sections and construction details in-house, including the insulation schedule and U-value calculations.
  3. ApplicationBuilding notice or full plans, submitted as your agent, with the drawing pack attached.
  4. Pre-start meetingWe meet the inspector on site before we start and agree the inspection stages and what evidence they want.
  5. Build and inspectionsFoundation, damp and pre-cover inspections arranged and attended, with photographic evidence where the inspector prefers it.
  6. CompletionFinal inspection, certificate issued, and your paperwork handed over.
Questions

Things people ask us

Do I need planning permission for a garage conversion, extension or porch?
Many garage conversions, single storey extensions and porches fall under permitted development, but conservation areas, listed buildings, Article 4 directions and estate covenants all change that. We will tell you where you stand, but confirming consents is ultimately your responsibility as the homeowner.
Does a garage conversion, extension or porch need building regulations approval?
Almost always yes, even where planning is not needed. That is not optional and it will matter when you sell.
Who does the electrics and plumbing on a garage conversion or extension?
We use registered electricians and Gas Safe engineers for notifiable work and coordinate them as part of the programme. The certificates come to you.
Can you build an extension or conversion to my own architect's drawings?
Yes, and it often makes for a smoother job. We will still want to walk the details with you before we price it.
What is the difference between planning permission and building regulations?
Planning permission controls what you build and how it looks from outside, while building regulations control how it is built: structure, fire safety, insulation, ventilation and drainage. They are separate consents from different parts of the council. Plenty of garage conversions and extensions need no planning permission but still need building regulations approval, and skipping it causes problems when you come to sell.
Who deals with building control for a garage conversion or extension?
We do: we produce the drawings, submit the building notice or full plans application as your agent, meet the inspector on site before starting and arrange every inspection stage through to the completion certificate. You still pay the council's building control fee directly, which in Milton Keynes is £460 plus VAT for a garage conversion on full plans. The certificates come to you at the end.
Should I use a building notice or full plans application?
Full plans is the safer route for most extensions and garage conversions, because the drawings are checked and approved before work starts and problems are settled on paper. A building notice lets work start sooner without approved drawings, but the inspector judges compliance as the job goes, which carries more risk of changes on site. It also cannot be used where you are building over a public sewer.
Do I need an architect for a small extension or garage conversion?
Not necessarily: for a single storey extension or garage conversion we produce the plans, sections, construction details and U-value calculations in-house for building control. You may want an architect if the design is ambitious or planning is likely to be contested, and a structural engineer is needed wherever steel beams or unusual foundations come in. We tell you at the visit which of those your project needs.
What is a building regulations completion certificate and why does it matter?
A completion certificate is the council's or approved inspector's confirmation that the finished work meets building regulations, issued after the final inspection. Buyers' solicitors ask for it when you sell, and missing certificates can delay a sale or mean retrospective approval with opened-up work. We arrange the final inspection and hand you the certificate, along with the electrical and gas certificates.
Do you manage a building project from start to finish?
Yes, we run garage conversions, extensions and porches from the first survey to the completion certificate: drawings, the building control application, the programme, registered electricians and Gas Safe engineers, inspections and the final paperwork. You get one fixed price in writing and one point of contact rather than juggling separate trades. If a project needs a structural engineer or party wall surveyor, we say so at the visit.
