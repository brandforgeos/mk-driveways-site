It is all in the junctions

A porch is a straightforward structure attached to an existing wall at four or five awkward junctions. Where the roof meets the house. Where the render or brickwork is cut back and made good. Where the threshold meets the existing floor and the DPC. Where the rainwater goods change direction.

Every one of those is a place where water gets in if the detail is wrong, and none of them are difficult if you take the time.

We build oak frame, timber frame and brick porches on proper foundations, and we do the making-good ourselves rather than leaving you with a patch of bare blockwork and a phone number for a renderer.

Detail

What we build in

A porch that has been done properly looks like it came with the house. One that has not looks stuck on, usually because the render patch never matched and the roof line is not quite right.

  • Proper concrete foundations, not a slab thrown on the topsoil
  • Lead or proprietary flashing and cavity trays at the abutment
  • Threshold detailing that keeps the DPC clear
  • Render made good and colour matched to the existing
  • Rainwater goods reworked so the porch roof actually drains
  • Oak allowed to move — fixings and detailing that expect it

Questions

Things people ask us

Do I need planning permission for a porch?
Generally not if it is under 3 square metres, no more than 3m high and at least 2m from a boundary facing a highway — but conservation areas and flats are different. Worth confirming before you order materials.
Do building regulations apply?
A porch under 3 square metres at ground level, with the existing front door retained, is usually exempt. Take the door out or go bigger and the regulations apply.
Oak or softwood?
Green oak moves, splits a little and silvers over — that is what it is meant to do and it looks superb on the right house. Softwood is cheaper, needs painting and wants maintaining. Both work, they just look like different things.
Can you match my render?
Silicone render can be colour matched closely and we will patch to the nearest break rather than leaving a square. A perfect invisible match on weathered render is not always achievable and we will say so beforehand.
