What you should do while you wait:

- On a fast road, pull as far left onto the verge or into a lay-by as you safely can.

## Why batteries go flat

Knowing the cause helps you avoid the same thing next week. The most common reasons we see are:

- **Age.** Batteries wear out gradually. One that has been in the car for years has less capacity left and gives up on the first cold snap.
- **Short journeys.** Lots of short trips, such as the school run or a quick drive to the shops, don't give the alternator enough time to put back what starting the engine took out.
- **Sitting unused.** A car parked for a few weeks slowly drains its battery through alarms, trackers and the car's own systems.
- **Something left on.** Headlights, an interior light, a boot that didn't fully close, or a phone charger in some cars.
- **Charging faults.** If the alternator or its drive belt isn't working, the battery runs down while you drive and the warning light usually comes on.
- **Cold weather.** Low temperatures reduce how much power a battery can deliver just when the engine needs more to turn over.

## Tips to avoid another flat battery

- After a jump start, drive for a good while on a steady route rather than switching off after a few minutes.
- If the car will sit for weeks, try to run it on a decent drive now and then.
- Check lights, the boot and doors are properly closed before you leave the car.
- If the battery warning light comes on while driving, get it checked soon, as the car may stop when the battery runs out.
- If the battery is old and struggled last winter, replace it before the next one rather than waiting for it to fail.

## How scrapping your car works

1. **Get in touch.** Call or WhatsApp 07598 314366, or use the [contact form](/contact). Give us the registration, make, model, mileage and condition. Photos help.
2. **Get a price.** We tell you what we can pay and what it is based on.

7. **Tax refund.** Once the DVLA knows the vehicle has been scrapped, any full months of vehicle tax left are refunded to you automatically.

## Licensed and legal

Scrapping a car properly protects you. If a vehicle isn't disposed of through the right channels, you can stay recorded as the keeper and remain liable for it.

- We hold a **waste carrier licence** and a **scrap metal collector's licence**.
- Every vehicle goes to a licensed **Authorised Treatment Facility (ATF)**.
- The ATF issues the **Certificate of Destruction** and notifies the DVLA.
- We pay by **bank transfer**, as required by the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013.

Be wary of anyone offering cash for a scrap car. Since the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013, it has been illegal to pay cash for scrap vehicles.

A scrap car often hasn't moved for a while. Brakes seize, tyres go flat and batteries die. Our trucks deal with that:

- **Dollies and skates** let us roll a car with seized wheels or locked steering out of a garage or tight space.

Tell us if the car is in a garage, behind a gate, on soft ground or in a low car park, and we bring the right kit.

## What decides the price of your scrap car

Scrap car prices depend on:

- **Weight.** Most of the value is in the metal, so heavier vehicles are usually worth more.
- **Scrap metal prices.** These move with the market, so the price can change over time.
- **Make and model.** Some vehicles have parts that are in demand.
- **Completeness.** A car with its engine, gearbox, catalytic converter and wheels is worth more than one with parts missing.
- **Condition.** Heavy damage or stripped parts can lower the price.

We give you a price before collection. The [scrap car cost guide](/costs/scrap-car-collection) explains how it's worked out.

## When is a car worth scrapping?

There's no single rule, but scrapping is usually the sensible choice when:

- The repair bill to pass an MOT is more than the car would be worth afterwards
- The car has been written off by an insurer and you have the option to keep it
- The engine or gearbox has failed and a replacement isn't worth fitting
- Rust has reached structural areas, which is expensive and often uneconomical to put right
- It has sat unused for so long that brakes, tyres, battery and fluids all need attention
- It's costing you money in insurance or tax, or sitting on a SORN, without being used

If you are unsure, tell us what's wrong with it when you ask for a price. We will be straight with you about what it's worth to us, and you can compare that with what a garage says the repairs will cost.

Garages, dealers and fleets also use us to clear vehicles that aren't worth repairing, and we can invoice businesses by arrangement for the collection side of regular work.

## Your V5C and paperwork

If you have the V5C logbook, the ATF needs it. Keep the small section for selling or transferring to the motor trade as your own record. If you don't have the V5C, we can still collect with proof of ID showing you are entitled to scrap the vehicle. Tell us when you call so we know what to check.

## What to have ready

- Registration, make, model and mileage
- A description of the condition, and photos if possible
- Whether it runs, rolls and steers
- Where it is parked and any access issues
