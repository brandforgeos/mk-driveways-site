## Is it time to scrap my car?

People usually decide to scrap a car for one of a few reasons:

- **The repair bill is too big.** A failed MOT with structural rust, a worn gearbox or a blown head gasket can cost more than the car is worth.
- **It has stopped running** and nobody wants to spend money finding out why.
- **It has been sat for years** on a drive or in a garage and is now in the way.
- **It was a second car** that is no longer needed.
- **It belonged to a relative** who has stopped driving or passed away, and the family needs it dealt with.

If you are not sure whether to fix it or let it go, our guide on [scrap or repair](/guides/scrap-or-repair) sets out the questions to ask.

The Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 made it illegal to pay cash for scrap metal, including scrap cars. Any "cash for cars" buyer is breaking the law. We pay by bank transfer when we collect, so you have a record of the sale and the money arrives before we drive away.

The law requires us to check the seller's name and address, so please have:

- Photo ID, such as a driving licence or passport
- Proof of address
- The V5C logbook, if you have it, and the keys

## Advisories, rust and the slow slide to scrap

Many cars do not fail all at once. The advisories build up over two or three tests: corrosion on a brake pipe, a slight oil leak, a tyre near the limit, some surface rust on a subframe. Each year the list gets longer, until the pass becomes a fail and the repair bill arrives in one go.

If you can see that coming, it can be worth selling before the next test rather than after. The car is the same weight either way, but you avoid paying for a test and repairs you were never going to finish. ## Damaged cars and your insurer

If you are still in a claim, check with your insurer before you sell. If the insurer has already paid out and you kept the car as a buy-back, the car is yours to sell. If the claim is still open, the insurer may want to inspect the car or may expect to take it themselves. We cannot advise on your policy, but it is worth a quick call to them before you accept a scrap price.

Keep any photos you took at the scene and the dashcam footage. Once the car has been scrapped, it cannot be inspected again. Not every damaged car is accident damage, of course. If yours has simply stopped running after years of standing, see our [non-runner collection](/non-runner) page instead.
