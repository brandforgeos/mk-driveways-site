## Why we don't publish a price list

People often ask us for a simple chart of what each car is worth. We don't give one, because no honest scrap car buyer can. Two cars of the same make and age can be worth quite different amounts once you look at their weight, what is still fitted and where they are parked.

What we do instead is give you a real figure for your own car. You enter the registration and postcode on our [instant price page](/get-a-price), we look the vehicle up, and you see a price on screen that is based on that car, not an average.

## How much is my scrap car worth, and what decides it?

The short answer is that a scrap car is valued mainly as metal. Scrap is bought and sold by the tonne, so a heavier car usually carries a higher value than a lighter one. On top of that, the figure moves with the scrap metal market, which goes up and down over the weeks and months.

After weight and the market, the details of the car matter. Is the engine still in? Is the gearbox there? Has the catalytic converter been taken off? Are there keys? Do you have the V5C logbook? Each of those nudges the price one way or the other. If you want the full background, our guide on [how scrap car prices work](/guides/how-scrap-car-prices-work) goes through it in detail.

## The weight question

Weight is where most of the value comes from. A small city car and a large estate or 4x4 can sit side by side on a driveway and be worth very different amounts, purely because one carries far more steel, aluminium and other metals than the other.

When we look up your registration, we can see the vehicle's details, including its make, model and size. That is how the instant price is able to reflect the real car rather than a rough guess.

## Why a complete car is worth more

A complete car has all the parts that give it value as scrap. Engines and gearboxes are heavy, and the catalytic converter contains precious metals. If any of those have been removed, there is simply less to recycle.

So if you have already taken parts off, or someone else has, tell us when you fill in the price form. That way the figure you see matches what we will find when we arrive. If the car is exactly as described, the price stands.

## Does it matter where the car is?

Yes, to a degree. We give free collection, so you never pay us to take the car away. But the postcode you enter is part of how the price is worked out, because travel is a real cost for us. We cover Corby, Kettering and the towns and villages around them, and we go further afield by arrangement.

Awkward spots are rarely a problem. Our collections are carried out with our own recovery trucks from Rapid Recovery Corby, so cars in tight spaces, low garages or with flat tyres can usually be moved without fuss.

## What a failed MOT means for the value

A car that has failed its MOT cannot legally be driven on the road, except to a pre-booked test or to a garage for the repairs. For many owners that changes the car overnight from transport into a problem on the driveway.

For us, the failure itself is not the main thing. We price an MOT failure in much the same way as any other end of life vehicle, starting with what it weighs and what is still fitted. The fact that it failed does not, on its own, knock the value down.

## How much is my MOT failure worth?

The figure is driven by the same core factors as any scrap car. Weight comes first, because scrap is priced by the tonne. The scrap metal market comes second, because that price moves over time. After that, the condition of the car and the paperwork you have fine-tune the number.

The quickest way to find out is to put your registration and postcode into our [instant price tool](/get-a-price). You will see a figure for your car, based on its real details.

## Why the failure reason rarely matters to us

A car might fail on corroded brake pipes, a worn suspension arm, structural rust on the sills or a dozen smaller items. To a garage, those are very different jobs. To a scrap buyer, the car still weighs the same and still has the same parts fitted.

That is why we don't ask you to send us the failure sheet before you get a price. The questions in our form focus on completeness, keys and paperwork, because that is what affects the figure.

## When an MOT failure might be worth more than scrap

Not every failed car should be scrapped. If the car is fairly new, in good order overall and has failed on a small, cheap item such as a bulb, a wiper blade or a tyre, it may be worth repairing and keeping or selling privately. In that case, scrap value could be well below what the car is worth on the road.

On the other hand, big failures change the sums. Structural rust, a failing gearbox, a worn clutch or a blown head gasket can cost more to fix than the car is worth. When repairs cost more than the car's value, scrapping is often the sensible option.

If you are on the fence, read our guide on [whether to scrap or repair your car](/guides/scrap-or-repair). It walks through the decision with a simple table, so you can be confident before you accept a price.

## What happens to value when a car is damaged

When a car is badly damaged, its value as a road car can drop sharply. Its value as scrap usually holds up much better. That is because scrap is priced mainly on weight, and a dented, crumpled or flood damaged car still contains most of its metal.

The things that really move a damaged car's scrap price are what has been lost or removed, and what the paperwork says about the car. This guide explains both.

## How much is my damaged car worth?

Your damaged car's value starts with its weight and the current scrap metal price per tonne. We then adjust for what is missing, whether the catalytic converter is still fitted, whether you have keys and the V5C, and the write-off category if there is one.

You can see a figure straight away by entering the registration and postcode on our [instant price page](/get-a-price). Answer the questions honestly about the damage and the price will reflect your car.

## Write-off categories explained

If an insurer has written your car off, it will be given a category. Each one says something different about what can legally happen to the car next:

- **Cat A**: the whole car must be crushed. Nothing can be salvaged.
- **Cat B**: the body shell must be crushed, but some parts can be salvaged.
- **Cat S**: structural damage. The car can be repaired and returned to the road.
- **Cat N**: non-structural damage. The car can be repaired.

If your insurer paid out and you kept the car (a buy-back), you can still scrap it with us. Tell us the category in the form so we have the full picture.

## Does the category change the price?

For a scrap car, the weight and completeness matter more than the label. A Cat N car with light panel damage and a Cat B car with heavy damage can still be valued in a similar way if both are complete and weigh about the same.

Where the category tends to matter is in what has happened since. Cars that have been partly stripped after a write-off, or that have lost parts in the accident, will be worth less because there is less to recycle.

## Flood damaged cars

Cars caught in floods on low roads are a common sight after heavy rain. Water can ruin the electrics, the engine and the interior, which often makes a repair uneconomic. As scrap, though, a flood damaged car still has its metal, so it is valued in much the same way as any other complete car.

If the car has been sitting in water or mud, let us know in the form. It helps us plan the collection.

## Why vans are valued differently from cars

A van is built to carry weight, so it is usually built heavier than a car of a similar length. Stronger chassis, bigger axles and larger body panels all add metal. Because scrap is priced by the tonne, that extra metal is the main reason vans can be worth more than cars as scrap.

We buy vans up to 3.5 tonnes, from small car-derived vans to long-wheelbase panel vans, Lutons and pickups. Business vans and ex-fleet vans are welcome.

## How much is my scrap van worth?

The value of your van comes mainly from its weight and the current scrap metal price per tonne. Size and body type are a big part of that weight. A small van and a long-wheelbase high-roof van are very different in how much metal they carry.

After weight, we look at completeness, the catalytic converter, keys and paperwork. Enter the registration and postcode on our [instant price page](/get-a-price) to see a figure for your van.

## Size, payload and body type

People often ask whether a van's payload affects its scrap price. Payload is how much a van is designed to carry, and it tends to go hand in hand with a heavier build. So a van with a larger payload rating is usually a heavier van, which generally means more value as scrap.

Body type matters for the same reason. A Luton box body or a dropside bed adds weight compared with a standard panel van of the same base. Pickups and 4x4s are often heavier than they look.

## What affects the price of a scrap van

| Factor | Effect on price |
| --- | --- |
| Size and wheelbase | Longer and taller vans weigh more and are usually worth more |
| Body type | Luton bodies, dropsides and pickup beds add weight |
| Payload rating | A higher rating usually means a heavier build and more metal |
| Scrap metal market | Moves over time, so van values rise and fall with it |
| Engine, gearbox and wheels | A complete van is worth more than a stripped one |
| Catalytic converter | Missing converters lower the price |
| Keys and V5C | Make loading and paperwork simpler |
| Location and access | Part of how the price is worked out. Collection is free |

## Empty the van first

Please clear the van of tools, materials, racking contents and rubbish before we collect. The price is for the van, not what is inside it, and loose items can be dangerous when a vehicle is loaded on a truck.

Fixed racking and ply lining can usually stay. If you are unsure about something bolted in, mention it in the form.

## Business and fleet vans

Tradespeople and small businesses often have an old van sitting on a unit or industrial estate after a replacement has arrived. We collect from business premises, yards and car parks as well as driveways.

Whoever sells the van needs to show photo ID and proof of address, as we are required to check the seller's name and address. If the van is registered to a business, have the V5C ready so the paperwork matches.

## Non-running and damaged vans

Vans that have failed an MOT, won't start or have been damaged are still priced mainly on weight. Our collections are carried out with our own recovery trucks from Rapid Recovery Corby, so a van with a seized engine or flat tyres can usually be moved. Tell us in the form if the van doesn't run so we can plan the right equipment.

## Will the price change on the day?

The price stands as long as the van matches what you told us. If it is different when we arrive, for example the catalytic converter has been removed, we explain why before loading and you can say no.

## Payment and paperwork

We pay instantly by bank transfer when we collect. The Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 makes it illegal to pay cash for scrap vehicles, so we never do.

Remember to remove any tracker, dashcam or fuel card, and cancel any toll or parking accounts linked to the registration. The van goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility, which issues a Certificate of Destruction and tells the DVLA. If you want to keep a private plate, transfer or retain it with the DVLA before the van is scrapped.

## What to have ready

To get an accurate price and a quick collection, have these to hand:

- The registration and the postcode where the van is parked.
- Details of anything missing, especially the engine, gearbox or catalytic converter.
- The keys and the V5C logbook.
- Photo ID and proof of address for the person selling.
- Any notes on access, such as a gated yard, barrier or height limit.

## Older and high mileage vans

A van that has covered huge mileage or spent years on site work is still valued mainly on its weight. Worn interiors, dented panels and tired engines don't change the metal content much. What matters is whether the major parts are still fitted and whether the paperwork is in order.

The same goes for vans with no MOT or no tax. If the van is on a SORN and kept off the road, that is fine. We collect it from where it sits, so it never needs to be driven.

## Vans sat on hard standing or in yards

Vans that have been parked in a yard or behind a unit for a long time can end up blocked in by other vehicles, pallets or skips. Clearing a path to the van before the agreed collection time saves time on the day and keeps the job safe.

## Two vehicles at once

Some of our trucks carry one vehicle and tow a second, so if a business has a van and a car to clear, both can often go in a single trip. Mention it in the form or when we call, and we will plan the collection around it.
