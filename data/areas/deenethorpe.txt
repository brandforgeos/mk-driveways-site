## Why rural car batteries go flat

**Cold, exposed parking.** Cars parked outside in open countryside get the full force of cold nights and frost. A cold battery delivers less power, and a tired one may not start the engine at all.

**Vehicles that are rarely used.** A second car, a weekend car or a farm runabout may sit for weeks. Batteries slowly drain when a car isn't driven.

**Short trips.** Quick runs to Weldon or Corby don't give the alternator long enough to recharge the battery properly.

**Age.** Older batteries lose capacity and eventually fail, usually on the coldest morning of the year.

If you are not sure the battery is the cause, our guide on [why a car won't start](/guides/car-wont-start) explains what to look for.

## Paid by bank transfer, as the law requires

The Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 bans cash payments for scrap metal, including scrap cars. We pay every scrap vehicle by bank transfer. That protects you and leaves a clear record of the sale.

## Licensed and done properly

We hold a waste carrier licence and a scrap metal collector's licence. Every vehicle we collect for scrap goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility (ATF). The ATF issues the Certificate of Destruction and notifies the DVLA.

Once the DVLA knows the vehicle has been scrapped, it refunds any full months of vehicle tax automatically. Many long-stored vehicles will already be declared off the road, but if yours is still taxed, you will get the refund without doing anything extra.
