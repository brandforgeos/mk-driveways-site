## Why batteries fail on the hill

Car batteries lose power in the cold. Add short trips around the village, cars left standing over winter, and modern cars drawing power while parked, and a weak battery soon runs flat. Common signs include:

- a slow, laboured crank when you turn the key
- clicking with no crank at all
- dashboard lights dimming or flickering
- the car starting after a jump, then failing again the next day

If the car starts after a jump but keeps going flat, the battery is probably past its best. We can test it and fit a replacement on the spot.

## Keeping your battery healthy through a Gretton winter

A few habits help a battery survive the colder months on the hill:

- Take a longer drive at least once a week, rather than only short trips to the shops.
- If a car will sit for weeks, consider a trickle charger on the drive or in the garage.
- Switch off headlights, heated screens and the blower before you turn the engine off.
- Book a battery check if the car is slow to start as the weather turns.

None of this guarantees the battery won't fail, but it reduces the chance of being stuck on a frosty morning. If it does go, we are a call away.

## Why we pay by bank transfer

We always pay for scrap cars by bank transfer. The Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 bans cash payments for scrap metal, and that includes scrap cars. Any business offering cash for a scrap car is breaking the law. A bank transfer also gives you a clear record of the sale.

## Licences and paperwork

We hold a **waste carrier licence** and a **scrap metal collector's licence**. Your car goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility (ATF), which:

1. Issues the Certificate of Destruction.

Once the DVLA knows, any full months of vehicle tax are refunded to you automatically. Keep your paperwork until the refund arrives. Our guide on [how to scrap a car](/guides/how-to-scrap-a-car) walks through each step.
