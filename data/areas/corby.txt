## Why do batteries go flat?

Most flat batteries we see in Corby come down to a handful of causes:

- **Short journeys.** A trip from Danesholme to the shops and back doesn't give the alternator long enough to top the battery up.
- **Cold weather.** Low temperatures reduce how much power a battery can deliver, which is why winter mornings are our busiest for jump starts.
- **Lights or accessories left on.** An interior light or a phone charger left plugged in overnight can drain a weaker battery.
- **Cars left standing.** A car parked for a couple of weeks, perhaps while you are on holiday or working away, can lose charge.
- **Age.** Every battery wears out eventually, and an old one will let you down at some point whatever you do.

## After a jump start

Once we have started your car, keep it running. A good drive of half an hour or so on a mix of roads gives the battery a chance to recharge. Avoid switching off straight away at the end of your road. If the car won't start again the next morning, the battery is likely to be the problem and replacing it is the sensible fix.

Do not keep jump starting a battery that has failed. It puts strain on the charging system and leaves you exposed to another breakdown, possibly somewhere less convenient than your own drive.

## How much will I get for my scrap car?

We will give you a price on the phone. It depends on:

| Factor | Effect on price |
| --- | --- |
| Weight of the vehicle | Heavier vehicles contain more metal |
| Current scrap metal prices | These move up and down over time |
| Make and model | Some models have more recoverable value |
| Completeness | Missing parts such as the engine or catalytic converter reduce value |
| Condition | Overall state of the vehicle |

Collection is free. You don't pay us anything to take the car away. Our [scrap car cost guide](/costs/scrap-car-collection) explains how prices are worked out.

## Why do we pay by bank transfer?

The Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 makes it illegal to pay cash for scrap metal, including scrap cars. So we always pay by bank transfer, even for low-value vehicles. Anyone offering cash for a scrap car is breaking the law, and that can be a sign the car won't be disposed of properly.

## Licensed and legal

We hold a waste carrier licence and a scrap metal collector's licence. Every scrap car we collect goes to a licensed ATF. The ATF issues the Certificate of Destruction and notifies the DVLA, which is what formally ends your responsibility for the vehicle.

Once the DVLA knows the car has been scrapped, it automatically refunds any full months of vehicle tax left. You don't need to apply separately.

## Common reasons Corby drivers scrap a car

We hear the same stories often. A car fails its MOT with a list of work that costs more than the car is worth. A clutch or gearbox goes on the A6116 and the repair quote is too high. A second car has sat on the drive for a year with flat tyres and a dead battery. Or an older car has been damaged in a minor accident and the insurer won't pay out enough to fix it.

In all of these cases, scrapping the car through a licensed route gets it off your drive, puts some money in your account and closes your responsibility for it properly.

## Things to avoid when scrapping a car

- Don't sell to anyone who offers cash. It is illegal for scrap, and it may mean the car won't reach an ATF.
- Don't hand over the car without being sure it is going to a licensed facility that will issue a Certificate of Destruction.
- Don't leave personal items, private documents or parking permits in the car.
- Don't strip valuable parts before calling for a price. An incomplete car is usually worth less.
