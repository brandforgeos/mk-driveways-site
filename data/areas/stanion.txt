## Failed MOT, what now?

Once a car fails, you have a few choices. You can pay for the repairs and retest, sell the car on, or scrap it. Which is right depends on the car and the failure.

Small items like bulbs, wiper blades or a tyre are worth fixing. The big ones are different. Structural rust, a worn clutch, gearbox problems and a failed head gasket can each cost more than an older car is worth. Our [scrap or repair guide](/guides/scrap-or-repair) helps you compare the two.

## Why village cars fail their MOT

In a small village like Stanion, a lot of driving is short hops into Corby or along the A6116, with the car sitting still in between. That pattern is hard on a car:

- Brakes corrode and stick when they are not used much.
- Exhausts rust out because they never get hot enough to dry.
- Batteries struggle and electrical faults appear.
- Suspension and underbody rust creep up year by year.

Advisories that were noted last year often turn into fails this year. When several land at once, scrapping becomes the obvious answer.

It helps to look back at your previous MOT certificates. If the same advisories have been carried for two or three years, the car has probably been waiting for this point. Have the fail sheet in front of you when you get our price, so you can set the repair quote against a firm figure.

## Write-off categories explained

When an insurer writes a car off, it gives it a category:

- **Cat A**: must be crushed entirely.
- **Cat B**: the body shell must be crushed, though some parts can be salvaged.
- **Cat S**: structural damage. It can be repaired and returned to the road.
- **Cat N**: non-structural damage. It can be repaired.

If the insurer has paid you out and you have kept the car, that is a buy-back. We can buy and scrap buy-backs.

## Should I repair a Cat S or Cat N, or scrap it?

Some owners keep a written-off car planning to fix it. For a Cat N with light damage, that can work. But structural repairs on a Cat S, airbag replacement, or damage to the suspension and steering can soon cost more than the car is worth. Once parts and labour pass the value of the car, scrapping it is the practical choice. Read our [scrap or repair guide](/guides/scrap-or-repair) before you decide.

- Ladders, roof racks and racking if they are yours to keep.
- Materials, waste and offcuts from the load area.
- Fuel cards, permits and paperwork from the cab.
- Dashcams, trackers and sat navs.

Anything left behind goes with the van. Cancel any fuel, toll or parking accounts linked to the reg. If the van has a private plate, transfer or retain it with the DVLA before it is scrapped.

## What is my scrap van worth?

Scrap is priced per tonne, so weight matters most, and vans are often heavier than cars. The current scrap metal price, the make and model, whether the van is complete with engine, gearbox, catalytic converter and wheels, and whether you have keys and the V5C all play a part, along with where the van is.

| Detail | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Size and weight | Heavier vans usually fetch more |
| Complete with engine and gearbox | Adds value |
| Catalytic converter fitted | Missing one reduces the price |
| Keys and V5C | Help with value and paperwork |

The [scrap van cost guide](/costs/scrap-van) and our guide on [how scrap car prices work](/guides/how-scrap-car-prices-work) explain more.

## Paperwork and payment

The seller needs photo ID and proof of address, because the law requires us to check them. Bring the V5C if you have it. If the van is owned by a business, mention it when we call so you know what to have ready. We pay by bank transfer when the van is loaded. Cash payment for scrap vehicles is illegal under the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013.

The van goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility, which issues a Certificate of Destruction and tells the DVLA. Any full months of tax are refunded automatically.

## Booking a time

We agree a collection time that suits you when we ring. Same-day collection is often possible but never guaranteed. Some of our trucks can carry one vehicle and tow another, which helps if you have a van and a car to go together.
