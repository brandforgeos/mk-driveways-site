## Is it a flat battery?

Here is a quick way to tell:

| What you notice | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Clicking, but the engine does not turn | Flat or failing battery |
| Engine turns slowly then stops | Weak battery, especially on cold mornings |
| No lights or dashboard at all | Completely flat battery or a loose connection |
| Engine turns normally but won't fire | Probably not the battery, may be fuel or ignition |

Our [car won't start guide](/guides/car-wont-start) has more on reading the signs.

## Why village cars lose their charge

Stanion drivers tend to do one of two things. Some make short trips into Corby and back, which do not give the alternator time to recharge the battery. Others leave a second car parked for long periods while one car does all the work. Either way, the battery slowly runs down, and a cold snap is often what finally stops it starting.

Interior lights, boot lights or accessories left on overnight are another regular cause.

## Paid by bank transfer, by law

The Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 bans cash payments for scrap metal, including scrap cars. Any car scrapper offering cash is breaking the law. We pay by bank transfer every time, which also gives you a clear record of the sale.

## What happens to your car

We hold a waste carrier licence and a scrap metal collector's licence. After collection, your car goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility (ATF). The ATF:

- Processes the vehicle in line with the regulations.
- Issues the Certificate of Destruction.
- Notifies the DVLA that the vehicle has been scrapped.

Once the DVLA has been notified, any full months of vehicle tax left on the car are refunded to you automatically.

## Checklist for collection day

- The V5C logbook, if you have it.
- All keys you have for the car.
- Your bank details for the payment.
- Personal items removed, including from the boot, door pockets and glovebox.
- Private plates dealt with before collection if you want to keep them.
