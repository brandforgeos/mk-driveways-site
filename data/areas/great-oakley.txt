## Is it worth fixing or should I sell it?

That depends on what failed. Bulbs, wipers and tyres are usually cheap enough to sort. The failures that tip a car towards scrap are the expensive ones: rust on structural parts, a worn clutch, gearbox trouble or a head gasket. When the repair bill is more than the car is worth, scrapping it is normally the sensible choice.

Our guide on [scrap or repair](/guides/scrap-or-repair) walks through the decision. If you are on the fence, get our price first so you have a firm figure to compare against the garage quote.

## Common MOT failures we see around Great Oakley

Many of the MOT failures we collect from Great Oakley are older second cars that do short local runs into Corby and Kettering. Short trips and long spells parked up are hard on a car. Typical reasons for failure include:

- Corrosion on sills, subframes or brake pipes.
- Suspension springs and arms worn or broken.
- Advisories that have built up year after year until they became failures.
- Emissions problems or warning lights on the dash.
- Brakes that have seized or worn unevenly from standing.

Keep the failure sheet handy when you get your price. It helps you compare the cost of the work with what we will pay, and it tells you whether you are looking at one big job or a long list of smaller ones that will keep coming back.

## Which write-off categories do you buy?

Insurers sort written-off cars into categories. Here is what each one means:

| Category | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Cat A | Must be crushed entirely |
| Cat B | Body shell must be crushed, some parts can be salvaged |
| Cat S | Structural damage, can be repaired and returned to the road |
| Cat N | Non-structural damage, can be repaired |

If your insurer has paid out and you kept the car, that is called a buy-back. You can still scrap a buy-back with us. Many Great Oakley owners keep the car thinking they will repair it, then find the parts and labour add up to more than it is worth.

## Is my damaged car worth repairing?

Sometimes, yes. A Cat N with light panel damage can be worth putting right. But once the damage reaches the structure, the airbags have gone off, or the repair estimate climbs past the value of the car, scrapping is usually the better route. Our [scrap or repair guide](/guides/scrap-or-repair) explains how to weigh it up.

## Why trade vans end up being scrapped

Working vans cover big miles with heavy loads, often on short stop-start runs. Clutches, gearboxes, suspension and rust on the floor and sills all take a beating. When the bill to keep a van going is more than it is worth, a lot of businesses around Great Oakley decide it is time to replace it and scrap the old one.

If you are not sure which way to go, read our guide on [whether to scrap or repair](/guides/scrap-or-repair).

## Collecting from industrial units and business premises

We regularly collect from business premises, yards and car parks as well as homes. If your van is at a unit on Oakley Hay, tell us about any gate codes, opening times or who on site we should speak to. If it is parked on the street outside your house in the village, that is fine too.

Our collections use Rapid Recovery Corby trucks, so a van that will not start, has a flat tyre or has seized brakes can still be loaded. We agree a time that suits you, and same-day collection is often possible but not promised.

## Empty the van first

This is the big one with vans. Before we arrive, please clear everything out:

- Tools, ladders and racking you want to keep.
- Materials, offcuts and waste from the load area.
- Paperwork, fuel cards and parking permits in the cab.
- Dashcams, trackers and sat navs.

Anything left in the van goes with it. Also cancel any toll, fuel or parking accounts linked to the registration, and if the van has a private plate, transfer or retain it with the DVLA before it is scrapped.

## How much is my scrap van worth?

Vans are usually heavier than cars, and weight is the biggest factor in a scrap price because scrap is priced per tonne. We also take into account the current scrap metal price, the make and model, whether the van is complete with its engine, gearbox, catalytic converter and wheels, whether you have keys and the V5C, and where it is.

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| How big is the van? | Bigger vans weigh more |
| Is the engine and gearbox still in? | Complete vans are worth more |
| Is the catalytic converter fitted? | Missing parts reduce the price |
| Do you have keys and the V5C? | Both help the price and the paperwork |

Our [scrap van cost guide](/costs/scrap-van) goes into more detail.

## Paperwork for a business van

Whoever is selling the van needs to show photo ID and proof of address, as the law requires us to check them. Bring the V5C if you have it. If the van is registered to a company, let us know when we call so we can talk through what to have ready. We pay by bank transfer when we load, and the van goes to an Authorised Treatment Facility, which issues a Certificate of Destruction and notifies the DVLA. Our guide to [how to scrap a car](/guides/how-to-scrap-a-car) covers the same steps for vans.
