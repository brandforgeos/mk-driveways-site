## Fix it or scrap it?

There is no single answer, but a few questions help:

1. Is the repair bill more than the car is worth?
2. Is the failure on something structural, like corroded sills or chassis rails?
3. Has the car got a big mechanical fault, such as the gearbox, clutch or head gasket?
4. Were last year's advisories warning about the same problems?

If you answer yes to the first and any of the others, scrapping is often the sensible choice. Our [scrap or repair guide](/guides/scrap-or-repair) goes into more depth.

Small fails are a different story. A blown bulb, a worn wiper or a single tyre is usually worth putting right, and we would rather tell you that than take a car that has years left in it. The cars that suit scrapping are the ones where the list keeps growing and every fix uncovers another job.

It also helps to think about the year ahead. Even if you pay for this year's repairs, an older car with tired suspension and creeping rust is likely to be back on the ramp with a fresh list next time.

## Advisories that caught up

A lot of the MOT failures we buy around Harringworth have a history. The car scraped through last year with a list of advisories about rust or worn parts, and this year those advisories became fails. Cars that spend their lives on country lanes, with mud, salt in winter and farm tracks, can see corrosion underneath move on quickly.

## When does a van become scrap?

A van stops making sense when it costs more to keep on the road than it earns. Typical signs are a failed MOT on corroded sills or chassis, a clutch or gearbox on the way out, or a new van arriving so the old one is surplus. A spare van sitting in a yard untaxed still needs a SORN, so scrapping it also clears that admin away.

Our [scrap or repair guide](/guides/scrap-or-repair) sets out the sums if you are undecided. For many trades, the answer comes down to downtime as much as the repair bill, because a van off the road for weeks costs work.

## Is my van worth more than a car?

Often, because vans are heavier, and scrap is priced per tonne. The current scrap metal price matters too, along with whether the van is complete with engine, gearbox, catalytic converter and wheels, the make and model, and whether you have the keys and V5C.

Our [scrap van cost guide](/costs/scrap-van) has more on pricing. Your online price stands as long as the van matches what you told us. If something is different on the day, we explain before we load.

## Business vans and paperwork

A van in a company name can still be scrapped. The person handing it over needs photo ID and proof of address, and the V5C helps. If you have lost it, our [guide to scrapping without a logbook](/guides/scrap-car-without-logbook) explains what to bring instead.

We pay instantly by bank transfer when we collect. Cash for scrap vehicles is illegal under the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013, and a transfer gives you a proper record for the books.

## What happens to the van

The van goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility, which issues a Certificate of Destruction, normally within 7 days, and notifies the DVLA. Keep the certificate with your business records. Full months of unused tax are refunded automatically. Cancel the insurance or take the van off your fleet policy.
