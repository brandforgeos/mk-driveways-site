## Three situations we see most

### Off the road on the descent

The lanes from Gretton down towards Harringworth are steep and exposed. In winter they can ice over, and a car that loses grip often finishes in the verge or the ditch. We use a winch to bring it back onto firm ground, then load it for recovery. Please don't try to drive it out yourself, as wheels spinning on wet grass can make things worse.

### Pulled in too far

On a single-track lane you sometimes have to tuck right into the side to let a tractor or van pass. Soft edges give way, and the car drops onto its sills. A short winch pull is usually all it takes, but it needs doing carefully to avoid damage.

### Mechanical breakdowns

Clutches, cooling systems and batteries fail here as they do anywhere. The difference is that a stopped car on a narrow lane leaves little room around it. We work quickly and keep our lights on so other road users can see us.

## Is it safe to wait in the car?

Usually it is safer to wait outside, away from the road, if you can do so safely. On a narrow lane:

1. Move the car as close to the side as you safely can, or into a gateway if one is right there.
2. Switch on your hazards, and your sidelights at night.
3. Get everyone out on the side away from oncoming traffic and stand well back.
4. Put on a high-vis if you have one.
5. If the car is stuck in the carriageway on a faster road and you can't get out safely, call 999.

If a car has stalled in water, don't try to restart it, as that can badly damage the engine. Call us instead. More detail is in our [what to do if you break down](/guides/what-to-do-if-you-break-down) guide.

## Flat battery in Harringworth? Here is what happens

A jump start in Harringworth is often all it takes to get you moving again. Rapid Recovery Corby comes out with professional jump packs and a supply of batteries, any hour of the day or night. In many cases you drive away with no tow needed.

Harringworth is a quiet village with a lot of cars that sit for days between trips. That is hard on batteries, and it is one of the most common reasons we get called to the valley.

## Why batteries go flat in a village like this

### Short or occasional journeys

If the car mostly does short runs, the alternator never fully recharges the battery. After a week of stop-start use, a cold morning can be the last straw.

### Cars parked for a while

Second cars, weekend cars and vehicles left while someone is away slowly drain. Modern cars draw a small current even when locked.

### Cold valley mornings

The Welland valley can be cold and damp, and the exposed lanes up towards Gretton freeze in winter. Batteries lose power in the cold just when the engine needs more to start.

### Visitors parked up

People stopping to look at the viaduct or walk nearby sometimes return to find the lights were left on. If that is you, tell us where you are parked and we will find you.

## What can you do at the roadside?

- **Jump starts** with professional packs. We check the charging once the engine is running.
- **Mobile battery fitting.** If the battery is past saving, we supply and fit a new one where the car is parked.
- **Wheel changes.** We fit your spare or space saver so you can reach a tyre fitter.

We do not offer lockout or wrong-fuel services.

## Is it the battery?

Not every non-start is a battery problem. A rough guide:

| What you notice | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| Engine turns slowly, lights dim | Weak battery |
| Fast clicking, nothing else | Battery very flat or a poor connection |
| Engine turns over normally but won't run | Fuel, ignition or sensor fault |
| Starts after a jump, dead again next day | Worn battery or charging problem |

Our [car won't start guide](/guides/car-wont-start) explains these symptoms in more depth. If it turns out to be something a jump can't fix, we can arrange [breakdown recovery in Harringworth](/breakdown-recovery/harringworth) straight away.

## While you wait

1. Switch off headlights, the heater fan and anything plugged in.
2. If you are on a lane, wait off the road with hazards on if the battery allows.
3. Find the locking wheel nut key if a tyre is the problem.
4. Note any warning lights that came on before the car died.

Share a WhatsApp live location or what3words reference when you call. On unlit lanes it makes finding you much easier.

## Should I jump start it myself?

You can, if you have leads, a second car and a safe place to do it. On a narrow lane with traffic, it is safer to wait. Connecting leads in the wrong order, or to a damaged battery, can cause harm. If you are unsure, call us.

## Keep your battery healthy

- Give the car a longer drive at least once a week.
- Turn off lights and heated screens before switching off the engine.
- Use a trickle charger if the car will sit for weeks.
- Have the battery tested before winter if it is a few years old.

## A flat battery in the cold, on your own

Being stuck with a dead car on a dark valley lane is unsettling, particularly if you are on your own or have children with you. Stay in a safe place, keep your phone charged if you can, and let someone know where you are. We always answer the phone, so you can call back for an update at any point while we are on the way.

If the car is parked somewhere awkward, such as a narrow verge or a field gateway, tell us. We would rather know before we arrive so we can position the truck safely and work without blocking the lane for longer than needed.

## Why we pay by bank transfer only

The Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 makes it illegal for scrap metal dealers and collectors to pay cash for scrap, and that includes cars. So we always pay by bank transfer. It also gives you a clear record of the sale.

## The paperwork side

We hold a **waste carrier licence** and a **scrap metal collector's licence**. Once we collect your vehicle, it goes to a licensed ATF. The ATF:

1. Issues a Certificate of Destruction.
2. Tells the DVLA the vehicle has been scrapped.

When the DVLA has been notified, any full months of vehicle tax left are refunded to you automatically. Our [how to scrap a car](/guides/how-to-scrap-a-car) guide walks through every step.

Q: Can I scrap my car without the V5C?
A: Yes. We can collect without a logbook as long as you show proof of ID.
Q: Why won't you pay cash for my scrap car?
A: The Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 bans cash payments for scrap, including scrap cars. We always pay by bank transfer.
Q: Will I get my car tax back?
A: Yes. Once the Authorised Treatment Facility tells the DVLA, any full months of tax are refunded automatically.
Q: How much will I get for my scrap car?
A: It depends on weight, scrap metal prices, make, model, completeness and condition. Call with the registration for a quote.
