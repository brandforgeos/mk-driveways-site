## Failed MOT in Weldon? What now

When a car fails its MOT, you have three choices: pay for the repairs, sell it to someone who will fix it, or scrap it. ## Is it worth repairing?

That depends on what failed and what the car is worth. As a rough rule, if the repairs cost more than the car, scrapping is usually the sensible choice. Big failures that often tip the balance include:

- Rust on structural parts such as sills, floor pans or suspension mounts.
- Gearbox or clutch problems.
- A failing head gasket.
- Several advisories that have piled up over a couple of years and have now turned into fails.

Our [scrap or repair guide](/guides/scrap-or-repair) walks through how to weigh it up. If you decide to repair, that's fine. If not, we are here.

## Can I drive an MOT failure to you?

You don't need to, and in most cases you shouldn't. A car without a valid MOT can generally only be driven to a pre-booked test or to have failure items repaired, and if the fault is dangerous it shouldn't be driven at all. We collect free from your drive, the roadside or a garage forecourt, so there is no reason to risk it.

## What do the write-off categories mean?

If your insurer wrote the car off, it will have been given a category. Here is what each one means:

| Category | What it means |
| --- | --- |

## Vans in a working village

Weldon has always been home to plenty of tradespeople, and the A43 makes it a handy base for working across Corby, Kettering and up towards Stamford. That means a lot of vans, and a lot of vans that eventually wear out. Big mileage, heavy loads, constant stop-start and years of being parked on the street all take their toll.

The vans we most often buy in Weldon are:

- A builder's or plumber's van replaced with a newer model.
- A pickup that has finally rusted through on the chassis or the load bed.
- A Luton used for a business that has closed or changed.
- An ex-fleet van bought cheap and now needing more than it is worth.
- A van that failed its MOT on structural rust or the clutch.

## Empty the van first

Before we collect, please take everything out. Tools, materials, racking you want to keep, ladders on the roof, and any paperwork in the cab. We can't take responsibility for anything left inside. Also:

1. Remove any tracker or dashcam you want to keep.
2. Take off company signage if you need to.
3. Transfer any private plate with the DVLA before collection.
4. Cancel fuel cards, toll accounts and parking accounts linked to the reg.

## How much is my scrap van worth?

Vans are generally heavier than cars, and weight is the main driver of scrap value along with the current scrap metal price. Make and model, whether the van is complete with engine, gearbox, catalytic converter and wheels, and whether you have keys and the V5C all affect the figure too. ## Collecting from yards and business premises

We collect from driveways and roadsides in the village, and from business premises and yards too. Our recovery trucks, run through our sister company Rapid Recovery Corby, include beavertail transporters with winches, so a van that won't start can still be loaded. Let us know if access is through a gate or a tight entrance.

Some of our trucks can carry one vehicle and tow a second, so if you are clearing two old vans from a yard, or a van and a car from the same address, tell us about both. They may be able to go in one trip.

## Selling a business van

If the van belongs to a company, the person selling needs the authority to do so, and we still check photo ID and proof of address as the law requires. Payment goes by bank transfer, so agree in advance which account it should go into. We never pay cash, because the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 makes cash payments for scrap metal illegal.

## Scrapping it properly

Every van we collect goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility. The ATF issues a Certificate of Destruction and tells the DVLA, and any full months of tax are refunded automatically. Keep the Certificate of Destruction as proof the van is no longer your responsibility. Our [Certificate of Destruction guide](/guides/certificate-of-destruction) explains why it matters.
