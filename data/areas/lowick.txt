## The legal side, kept simple

We hold a waste carrier licence and a scrap metal collector's licence. Every car goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility, known as an ATF. The ATF issues a Certificate of Destruction, normally within 7 days, and notifies the DVLA. Keep that certificate as proof you are no longer responsible for the car. Once the DVLA knows, any full months of vehicle tax left are refunded to you automatically. You should cancel the insurance yourself.

## Collection from yards and business premises

We collect from driveways, farm yards, roadsides and business premises. Vans parked in a yard for months often have flat tyres or seized brakes. We collect with recovery trucks from our sister company, Rapid Recovery Corby, with winches and dollies to move vans that will not roll. Tell us about access, gates and ground conditions when we call.

If a business has two vehicles to clear, let us know. Some of our trucks carry one and tow another.

## Paperwork for business vans

The person selling needs photo ID and proof of address, as we are required to check by law. If the van is registered to a business, have the V5C and something showing your connection to the business. Without a V5C you can still sell with proof of ID and ownership.

Every van goes to a licensed Authorised Treatment Facility. It issues a Certificate of Destruction and notifies the DVLA. Keep the certificate for your business records. The [certificate of destruction guide](/guides/certificate-of-destruction) explains why it matters.

## Before we arrive

1. Clear all tools, stock and materials.
2. Remove trackers, dashcams and fuel cards.
3. Cancel any toll or parking accounts linked to the registration.
4. Transfer a private plate with the DVLA if you want to keep it.
5. Cancel the insurance after collection.

## Payment by bank transfer

We pay by bank transfer at collection. Cash payments for scrap vehicles have been illegal since the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013. For a business, a bank transfer also gives you a clean record of the disposal for your accounts.

## Replacing a van rather than repairing it

Many trades reach the point where an ageing van costs more in repairs and downtime than it earns. If it has failed its MOT on rust, needs a clutch or gearbox, or simply will not start on a cold morning, scrapping it frees up the yard for the replacement. Our [scrap or repair guide](/guides/scrap-or-repair) covers how to weigh it up.
