Glass verandas in Wolverton: what is involved

Wolverton is a Victorian railway town — the London & Birmingham Railway built its works here from 1838, and the carriage works later built the Royal Train. In the grid of terraces many houses sit right on the pavement with no front garden, later streets have a low brick wall and a small yard, and back ways serve the rear.

A veranda is an aluminium-framed roof fixed to the back wall of the house, with clear glass or polycarbonate panels and a built-in gutter, so you can sit out in the rain and the kitchen still gets its light.

Ground and conservation in Wolverton

The town sits on limestone of the Great Oolite group — the local Blisworth stone — under patches of boulder clay, with the Great Ouse flood plain to the north. Do I need planning permission for a veranda?
Usually not if it is at the back of the house and within the rear extension limits. In a conservation area or on a listed house, the rules are tighter and we check first.
Glass or polycarbonate?
Glass looks better, stays clearer and is quieter in rain; polycarbonate is lighter and cheaper. Both keep you dry.
