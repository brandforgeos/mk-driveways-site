Questions

Things people ask us

Is a porcelain patio slippery when wet?
External porcelain is made with a textured face and rated for wet grip. It is generally less slippery than smooth sawn stone.
Can a porcelain patio be laid over an old patio?
Sometimes, if the old one is sound and the levels work at your door. We check rather than assume.
What are the white marks that appear on new porcelain and stone patios?
Efflorescence - salts coming out of the bedding in the first winter. It washes off and porcelain shows it far less than stone.
What thickness of porcelain is best for a patio?
20mm vitrified porcelain is the standard for outdoor patios, and it is what we lay. It is strong enough for garden use when it is fully bedded, frost-proof and almost non-porous. Thinner 10mm tiles are made for indoors or for fixing onto a concrete slab with adhesive, and they are not suitable for laying on a mortar bed in a garden.
What slip rating should outdoor porcelain paving have?
Look for outdoor porcelain rated R11 on the ramp test, or a wet pendulum test value of 36 or above, which is classed as low slip risk. Most 20mm external porcelain is made with a textured face to meet that. Polished or indoor porcelain does not, and should never go outside. Algae in shade makes any surface slippery, so a regular wash still matters.
How do you clean a porcelain patio?
Warm water and a stiff brush handle most of it, with a cleaner made for porcelain for anything stubborn. Because porcelain barely absorbs water, dirt sits on the surface rather than soaking in. A pressure washer on a low setting is fine for the slabs, but keep the jet off the joints so you do not blow out the pointing. Avoid acid cleaners unless the product says they are porcelain safe.
Does porcelain paving stain?
Very rarely. Porcelain is almost non-porous, so barbecue fat, red wine, leaves and rust marks from furniture generally sit on the surface and wipe or scrub off. The joints are more likely to mark than the slabs. The one thing that can be hard to shift is cement residue left on the face during laying, which is why the slabs should be cleaned as the job goes along.
Can porcelain paving crack or chip?
It can, though a properly laid porcelain patio is very hard wearing. Porcelain is dense but brittle, so a heavy object dropped on an edge can chip it, and a slab with voids under it can crack under load. Laying every slab on a full mortar bed, with the back primed, removes those voids. Keeping a few spare slabs from the job makes any future repair easy to match.
How long does a porcelain patio last?
A porcelain patio laid on a full bed over a proper sub-base should last 25 years or more. The colour is fired into the slab, so it does not fade, and the surface does not wear the way natural stone or concrete can. The joints are usually the first thing to need attention, and what usually ends a porcelain patio early is loose slabs from poor bedding.
What is the best jointing for porcelain paving?
Porcelain is normally laid with narrow joints of a few millimetres, filled with either a cementitious slurry grout or a brush-in jointing compound made for porcelain. Slurry grout is hard and tidy but must be cleaned off the face promptly; brush-in compounds are quicker and slightly flexible. Ordinary sand and cement pointing tends to crack in narrow porcelain joints. We agree the method and colour with you before laying.
Does porcelain paving get black spot or moss?
It can get green algae and some lichen in damp, shaded corners, but because porcelain is almost non-porous it cannot root into the slab the way it does into sandstone, so it washes off with a brush. Moss and weeds will try to grow in the joints, which is why a full, well-compacted joint matters. A wash once or twice a year keeps a porcelain patio looking new.
