Ground, drainage and conservation in Hanslope

Boulder clay over Blisworth limestone means the top layer is usually clayey and slow to drain, so we test before designing a soakaway and allow for clay movement near trees. On clay we dig deeper, lay a geotextile under the sub-base and decide exactly where the water goes. Tarmac is impermeable, so the falls, a channel drain and a working outlet are designed in from the start.

Hanslope Conservation Area’s review says boundaries are generally stone or brick walls, close-boarded or panel fencing is normally resisted, and parking should avoid harming front gardens and verges. There is no Article 4 direction on unlisted houses.

There is no main river here; Milton Keynes City Council’s flood risk assessment notes a cluster of sewer flooding records in Hanslope. You can check any address on the Environment Agency flood map.
