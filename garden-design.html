The approach

Survey, levels, structure, surface, planting

A full garden is a construction project with plants at the end of it. We run it that way. Survey the existing ground and the levels. Work out where the terraces sit and how water leaves the site. Design the retaining, the steps and the drainage. Then the surfaces, the boundaries, the lighting and finally the planting.

Doing it in that order means the expensive structural decisions get made when they are still cheap to change — on paper — rather than when there is a digger on site.

You get one contract, one programme and one guarantee. We do not hand you off to three different trades and let you referee between them.

The process

How a garden project runs

  1. Site visit and surveyWe measure, take levels and photograph everything. For larger or awkward gardens we will sometimes fly a drone and work a scale plan up from the aerial image.
  2. Concept and proposalA written proposal with the scope, the construction details, a programme and one price. Visuals where they help you picture it.
  3. Deposit and drawingsOnce you accept and the deposit clears, we produce the setting-out drawings and order the materials from single batches.
  4. Groundworks and structureStrip out, dig, drainage, footings, retaining walls and steps. The longest and least photogenic part of the job.
  5. Surfaces and boundariesPaving, edges, screening and fencing. This is where it starts to look like the drawing.
  6. Lighting, soft landscaping and handoverLighting installed and commissioned, topsoil, turf and planting, then a full clean and your completion pack.
What we build into it

The parts you will not see

Every garden we build carries the same underlying work whether the brief mentions it or not.

  • Drainage designed so water leaves the site properly
  • Concrete footings under every wall and step
  • Ducting run for lighting and future power before anything is paved
  • Irrigation sleeves under hard surfaces where you might want them later
  • Levels set so no threshold ends up within 150mm of the DPC

Questions

Things people ask us

Do I need a separate garden designer for a design and build garden?
For most gardens, no. For a large or planting-led scheme, a designer adds real value and we are happy to build to someone else's drawings.
How much does a full garden design and build cost?
It depends mostly on the levels, the access and the materials. Our garden makeover cost guide gives honest 2026 ranges. What we can tell you is that groundworks and structure usually account for more of the budget than people expect, and paving less.
Can a garden design and build project be done in phases?
Yes, and often it should be. We will set out a sensible phasing so that the ducting, drainage and structure all go in first and later phases do not mean digging up finished work.
How does access affect a garden design and build project?
Rear gardens with only a narrow side gate change the cost significantly because everything comes through by barrow. We will look at that on the visit and price it honestly rather than discover it on day one.
How is garden design and build different from hiring a designer and a landscaper separately?
With garden design and build, one firm surveys, designs and builds the garden under a single contract, programme and guarantee, so nobody can blame the drawing for a build problem or the builder for a design one. Hiring a designer separately makes sense for large or planting-led gardens, but you then manage the handover between them. Because we design as builders, the construction details are worked out before the price is fixed.
How long does a garden design take before work can start?
The design and fixed price usually follow about three working days after the survey visit, and most people go through a version or two before settling on it. Once the design is accepted and the deposit clears, setting-out drawings are produced and materials ordered in single batches, so the start date then depends mainly on the diary and the lead times for the paving and walling you have chosen.
Will I get a scale plan or visuals of my garden design?
You get a layout drawn to your measurements showing the shape, levels and materials, with design visuals where they help you picture the finished garden. For larger or awkward plots we sometimes fly a drone and build the scale plan from the aerial image. Detailed setting-out drawings and sections follow once the job is booked, because that is when they are actually needed.
What should I think about before a garden design visit?
Before a garden design visit, think about how you want to use the space rather than what it should look like: where the sun is when you sit out, how many people you eat with, children, dogs, storage and how much maintenance you will realistically do. A rough budget helps us design something you can afford to build, and it is worth finding your deeds or estate paperwork if covenants or boundaries might matter.
Why does a garden design need a levels survey?
A levels survey shows how the ground actually falls, which decides where the terraces go, how many steps are needed, whether walls have to retain soil and where the water ends up. It also fixes the patio level against the house, which should finish at least 150mm below the damp proof course. Designing without levels is how gardens end up with patios that puddle, steps that do not fit and walls nobody priced.
Can a garden design allow for a hot tub, garden room or lighting later?
Yes, a garden design can allow for all three, and it costs very little at the design stage. We run ducting for lighting and future power, and irrigation sleeves under hard surfaces, before anything is paved, and can leave a level, suitably built area where a hot tub or garden room will go. Adding any of those after the garden is finished usually means lifting paving or digging through a new lawn.
