Scan comparison versus coring

Coring is the traditional thickness check, and adopting authorities still ask for some cores. A core gives you a physical sample and a true thickness at that spot. What it can't do is tell you about the 99.9% of the road between the cores.

CoringScan comparison
CoverageOne spot per coreThe whole surface
DamageHoles to reinstateNone
Finds where it's thinOnly if a core lands thereYes, mapped and measured
Needs planning aheadNoYes, the layer below must be scanned first

For most estate roads the best value is both: a scan comparison across the whole road, and a small number of cores placed where the scan says they're most useful.

A worked example

A 250 m estate road. We scan the sub-base as a level check before surfacing (£495), then scan the binder course after it's laid and produce the thickness report (£450). Total £945 for a level check on the sub-base and a full binder thickness map, with tonnage worked out from it.

Ways to keep it down

  • Book the sub-base scan as soon as surfacing is programmed. It's the one part you can't go back for.
  • Survey binder and surface course on the same road to share set-up and travel.
  • Group several roads on the same phase into one visit.

What's included

  • Both scans on the same site control
  • Colour thickness map over the layout
  • Min, average and max thickness
  • Areas outside tolerance in m²
  • Volume and tonnage estimate
  • Point clouds and registration errors

Questions we get asked

How much is a tarmac depth survey?

From £895 per road section for both scans (the layer underneath and the finished surface) and the thickness report. If we have already scanned the sub-base as part of a road scan, the surface scan and thickness report are from £450.

Is it cheaper than coring?

Per hole, a core is cheaper. But a core proves the thickness at one spot, and you need several per road plus reinstatement. A scan comparison covers the whole surface, tells you exactly where any shortfall is, and costs about the same as a modest coring programme on a typical estate road.

Can I get a price for tarmac already laid?

Only if the layer underneath was scanned or accurately levelled before it was covered. Without that, thickness can't be measured by scanning and coring is the only option.

What's a road section?

One continuous length of road scanned in a half-day visit, usually 200 to 300 metres. Longer roads are priced as additional sections or full days.
