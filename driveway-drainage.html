The rule in plain English

Where is the water going?

Since 2008, if you pave over a front garden with an impermeable surface and let the water run onto the road, you need planning permission. If the surface is permeable, or if the water is directed to a soakaway, a border or a lawn on your own land, you generally do not.

That is the whole of it. Everything else is detail about how you achieve it. What matters is that somebody actually thinks about it before the stone goes in, because retrofitting drainage into a finished driveway is an expensive and destructive job.

On a lot of Milton Keynes estates the ground is clay and the water does not go anywhere quickly, which is exactly when this needs designing rather than guessing.

Options

Five ways we deal with water

A fully permeable build-up

Permeable surface over open-graded stone. The whole driveway becomes the drainage system and there is nothing to block or maintain. Our default where the ground allows.

Soakaway

A stone-filled or crated void sized to hold a storm and let it out slowly. Needs a percolation test to size properly, and needs to be the right distance from foundations.

Channel drain

A linear drain across the threshold or at the low point, connected to a soakaway or a surface water connection. Simple, effective, needs occasional clearing.

Falls to a border

The cheapest answer where it works. Lay the drive falling away from the house into a planted border and let the ground take it.

Check baffles on a slope

On a long fall, dams within the sub-base hold water back in stages so it soaks in rather than sheeting to the bottom. Cheap to build in, impossible to add later.

Connection to surface water

Where a legitimate surface water sewer or existing connection exists, it may be the right answer — but never a foul drain, and never a neighbour's.

How we approach it

Design, not hope

Where drainage is critical we will dig a trial hole and run a percolation test rather than assume. It costs a morning and it is the difference between a soakaway that works and one that fills up in November and never empties.

The result goes into your proposal as a written drainage note, with the build-up drawn as a section. If it turns out your ground is free-draining sand and gravel, we will say so and save you the money on storage you do not need.

  • Trial hole and percolation testing where the design depends on it
  • Soakaways sized against the storm event, not by eye
  • Kept the required distance from foundations and boundaries
  • Levels set so water never runs towards the house
  • Existing gullies and chambers surveyed before we design anything
Questions

Things people ask us

Do I need planning permission for my new driveway?
Not if the surface is permeable, or if the water drains to your own land rather than the highway. If neither applies, you do. We will tell you which situation you are in at the visit.
What is a driveway soakaway and where can it go?
A void in the ground that holds surface water and lets it soak away slowly. It needs to be a safe distance from buildings and boundaries and it needs ground that will actually absorb water, which is why testing matters.
My driveway floods now. Will a new driveway fix it?
Usually, but only if we understand why it floods first. Sometimes it is a compacted crust on the surface, sometimes it is levels running the wrong way, and occasionally it is a blocked drain that has nothing to do with the driveway at all.
Can I drain my driveway into the foul sewer?
No. It is not permitted and it causes flooding elsewhere in the system. If a previous surface was connected that way we will tell you.
What are the options for draining a driveway?
There are five main ways to drain a driveway: build the whole drive permeable, lay it to fall onto a planted border or lawn, fit a channel drain to a soakaway, connect to a legitimate surface water sewer with approval, or on long slopes add check baffles in the sub-base. Permeable is our default where the ground takes water. On slow-draining clay the choice needs designing from a ground test rather than guessed.
How far does a soakaway need to be from a house?
A soakaway should normally be at least 5m from the house or any other building, and away from boundaries, so the water it releases does not soften the ground under foundations. Building regulations guidance also keeps soakaways away from roads and unstable ground. On a small Milton Keynes frontage that distance is often hard to find, which is one reason a permeable base or lined system can suit better than a single soakaway pit.
What is a percolation test and why does my driveway need one?
A percolation test measures how fast your ground absorbs water: a trial hole is dug, filled with water and the drop is timed. The result sets how big a soakaway needs to be, or shows it will not work at all. It takes about a morning, and it is the difference between a soakaway that copes and one that fills in November and stays full. If the ground turns out to be free-draining, the test saves you paying for storage you do not need.
Where should a channel drain go on a driveway?
A channel drain goes across the lowest point of a driveway or wherever water would otherwise reach the house or garage, most often across a garage door or at the bottom of a drive that slopes towards the property. It must be bedded and haunched in concrete, rated for car loads and connected to somewhere the water can legally go, such as a soakaway. A channel that just discharges onto the road defeats the point.
Can I connect my driveway drainage to the surface water sewer?
You can connect driveway drainage to a public surface water sewer only with the water company's approval, and never to a foul sewer or a neighbour's drain. An existing gully on the drive is not proof that its connection is legitimate either. Where the ground allows, soaking the water away on your own land is usually simpler and avoids the approval process altogether.
Do soakaways work on clay soil?
Soakaways often struggle on clay soil because clay takes water in very slowly, so a pit that works in summer can fill up in winter and stay full. Much of Milton Keynes and Bletchley sits on Oxford Clay, and Cranfield's boulder clay drains worse still, so soakaways there may need to be larger or replaced by a lined permeable base with a controlled outlet. On the Greensand at Woburn Sands and Aspley Guise they usually work well.
Which way should a driveway fall, and how steep should the fall be?
A driveway should always fall away from the house, garage and neighbouring walls, towards a border, a permeable area or a drain, with a fall typically between 1 in 40 and 1 in 80 depending on the surface, enough to shed water without looking like a slope. The finished level should also stay at least 150mm below the damp-proof course. Falls running back towards a threshold are one of the most common causes of damp and standing water.
Guide prices

What driveway drainage costs

A channel drain across a typical driveway or garage entrance costs about £1,000–£1,800 installed, or £120–£200 per metre for a heavy-duty, vehicle-rated channel. Soakaways are sized and priced after a percolation test.
