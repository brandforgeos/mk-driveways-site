## Accurate UK scrapping facts you can use
- Only an Authorised Treatment Facility (ATF) can legally scrap a car. The ATF gives a Certificate of Destruction, normally within 7 days, and tells the DVLA. Keep the CoD as proof you are no longer responsible for the vehicle.
- V5C: you hand over the logbook with the car. Keep the small yellow slip (the "selling to the motor trade" section). If you don't have the V5C you can still scrap the car with proof of ID and proof of ownership/address.
- Once the DVLA knows the vehicle is scrapped, any full months of vehicle tax left are refunded automatically. You should also cancel the car's insurance yourself.
- Personalised number plates: if you want to keep a private plate, you must transfer or retain it with the DVLA BEFORE the car is scrapped. Once scrapped, it is lost.
- SORN: a car with no tax kept off the road needs a SORN. A car that's been scrapped doesn't.
- Write-off categories: Cat A (must be crushed entirely), Cat B (body shell must be crushed, some parts can be salvaged), Cat S (structural damage, can be repaired and returned to the road), Cat N (non-structural damage, can be repaired). If your insurer has written your car off and you kept it (a buy-back), you can still scrap it with us.
- Before collection: remove personal belongings, check the glovebox, door pockets, boot and under the spare wheel, take any dashcams, sat navs and parking permits, and cancel any toll/parking accounts linked to the reg.
- Selling vs scrapping: if repairs cost more than the car is worth, or it has failed its MOT on big items (rust on structural parts, gearbox, clutch, head gasket), scrapping is often the sensible option.
- Never give legal advice beyond these facts.
