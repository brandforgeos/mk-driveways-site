Tarmac depth surveys

Tarmac depth surveys: the thickness laid, everywhere

One scan before the asphalt, one after. The difference is the thickness at every point across the road, mapped, measured and checked against your spec, with no cores and no guesswork.

Book a depth surveyTarmac survey prices

How it works: we laser scan the sub-base (or the binder) before it's covered, then scan again once the next layer is laid. Both scans sit on the same site control, so subtracting one surface from the other gives the compacted thickness at every point. The report shows a thickness map, the minimum, average and maximum, and the area under or over the tolerance.

Why thickness matters, and why cores only tell half of it

Asphalt is bought by the tonne and specified by the millimetre. Lay it thin and the road fails early, the adoption engineer rejects it, or the defect turns up in the maintenance period. Lay it thick and the extra tonnage comes straight off the margin, often without anyone noticing until the final account.

Cores are the usual check, but a core is one 150 mm hole in hundreds of square metres. It confirms the thickness where it was drilled and nowhere else. A scan comparison gives you the thickness everywhere, including the edges, channels and around ironwork where shortfalls tend to hide.

What the report includes

  • A colour thickness map of each bituminous layer over the road layout.
  • Minimum, average and maximum thickness, and the percentage of the area within tolerance.
  • Areas under the specified thickness outlined, with their size in m².
  • Approximate volume and tonnage laid, useful for checking deliveries against the take-off.
  • Surface level of the finished layer against design, from the same scan.
  • Point clouds and the registration error for both scans.

The one rule: book the first scan in time

A depth survey needs a "before" surface. Once the binder is down, the sub-base is gone for good. Let us know when the road is programmed and we'll scan the sub-base as part of a normal road and sub-base scan, which also checks it against design before you surface it.

Typical tolerances

Councils publish their own. As an example, Lincolnshire County Council's estate road guide allows ±6 mm on binder and surface course thickness and +40/−0 mm on sub-base thickness. We report against whichever applies to your road.

Questions we get asked

How do you measure tarmac thickness without coring?

We scan the layer underneath before the tarmac goes down and scan the finished surface afterwards. Both scans are tied to the same site control, so the vertical difference between them at any point is the thickness laid there. It is non-destructive and covers the whole area, not just the core locations.

Can you survey tarmac that's already been laid?

Only if the layer below was scanned or accurately levelled first. Without a 'before' surface there's nothing to measure the thickness from, and coring is the only option. That's why we recommend booking the sub-base scan as soon as the road is programmed.

Does this replace cores for adoption?

That's the adopting authority's call. Many accept full-coverage scan data alongside a reduced number of cores, and it shows them exactly where to core. We provide the data in a form their engineer can check.

How accurate is the thickness?

Each scan is registered to the same control and we state the registration error on the report. In practice the thickness measurement is good to a few millimetres, which is well inside a ±6 mm binder or surface course tolerance. Where the margin is tight, we say so.

Is it worth it on a small job?

On a single driveway or a short cul-de-sac, a few cores or dips may be enough. Scanning pays off on estate roads, car parks and anywhere a shortfall would be expensive to take up and relay.
