Why it is different

New-build plots need a different approach

We have spent years on the other side of this — building new housing estates. So we know what is usually under the turf, how the levels were set, and what the developer’s warranty needs you not to do.

  • Compacted clay and building debris under thin turf, which is why the lawn never drains
  • Levels set close to the damp proof course, so new paving has to be detailed carefully
  • Covenants and management companies that control front gardens on many estates
  • Narrow drives designed for one car, on plots that need two

Questions

Things new-build owners ask

Can I extend my new-build driveway over the front lawn?
Usually, but check two things first. Many Milton Keynes estates have covenants keeping front gardens open plan or controlling driveways, so look at your deeds or ask your management company. And if the new area drains onto the road it may need planning permission — we design it to drain on your own plot so it does not.
Will work on my garden affect my NHBC warranty?
Not if it is done properly. The things that cause problems are paving laid too high against the house, blocking air bricks, and water directed at the walls. We keep finished levels at least 150mm below the damp proof course and the falls away from the house.
Why is my new-build lawn always waterlogged?
Very often there is compacted clay and building debris a few inches under the turf. Re-turfing does not fix it. Taking the top layer out, breaking up the ground and putting in proper topsoil and drainage does.
When is the best time to book new-build garden or driveway work?
Book the visit over winter. Designs and prices are done in a few days, and spring diaries fill quickly once the weather turns.
How soon can I landscape a new-build garden?
You can landscape a new-build garden as soon as you have completed, but it often pays to wait until the developer has finished snagging and you have reported any defects, so there is no argument about who damaged what. Service trenches and backfill around the house can settle over the first winter, so we check for soft spots before building over them. Booking the design early means you are ready when that point comes.
Will the developer fix a waterlogged new-build garden?
Possibly, if you raise it within the first two years. NHBC Standards include requirements for garden areas close to the house, so serious waterlogging or buried builder's debris near the home is worth reporting to the developer in writing before you change anything. Gardens further from the house are much less protected, and once you have dug the area up a claim gets harder. If the developer will not act, it can be put right properly as part of the new garden.
Can I put up a fence in my new-build front garden?
Often not without checking first. Many Milton Keynes estates have open-plan front gardens, and the original planning permission or your transfer deed can remove the right to put up fences, walls or hedges in front of the house. Look at your title deeds, the management company's rules and the planning decision for the estate. Rear garden fences up to 2m are normally fine unless the same conditions say otherwise.
Do I need planning permission for a patio or garden room on a new-build?
A rear patio normally does not need planning permission, but on many new-build estates the planning consent removes some permitted development rights, most often for extensions, outbuildings and front boundaries. That can catch out garden rooms, raised decks and larger sheds. Check the planning decision notice for your estate on the council's planning portal, or we will look it up for your address at the free design visit.
How do I get privacy in an overlooked new-build garden?
Privacy on an overlooked new-build plot usually comes from a mix of height and planting: a slatted screen or trellis on the fence up to the 2m limit, a pergola over the seating area, and pleached or multi-stem trees that screen upstairs windows without blocking the light. Positioning the patio where it is least overlooked, rather than straight off the back doors, often does more than any screen. Covenants can limit fence heights, so check those first.
Can I build a patio over a manhole in my new-build garden?
You can pave around or over an inspection chamber, but the cover must stay accessible, so it is usually replaced with a recessed tray filled with matching paving. If the chamber or pipe serves more than one house it is likely to be a public sewer, owned by Anglian Water across Milton Keynes, and building a structure over or close to it needs their consent. A patio normally does not, but a garden room or extension usually does.
How much does it cost to landscape a new-build garden?
Landscaping a new-build garden in Milton Keynes usually costs £6,000–£12,000 for a refresh, such as replacing the builder's slab patio and relaying the lawn on proper topsoil, and £15,000–£35,000 for a full rear garden with levels, paving, beds, boundaries and lighting. New-build plots often need the compacted subsoil and debris under the turf dealing with, which is worth budgeting for rather than discovering halfway through.
How much does a new-build driveway extension cost?
A new-build driveway extension is priced per square metre in the material that best matches the house: block paving typically £120–£170 per m², permeable block £140–£210 and resin bound £150–£220. A standard parking space is roughly 2.4m by 4.8m, and on top of the surface the price depends on whether the dropped kerb needs widening, which needs highway authority consent, any covenant approval and how the new area drains.
